  - heading "Answers or actions?" [level=2]
  - paragraph: A knowledge assistant focuses on finding information and answering questions. An AI agent may also call tools, connect steps or take actions. Answering a question does not establish reliable order changes or stock updates.
  - paragraph: RAG retrieves relevant material before generating an answer. A more complex agent needs clear task steps, available integrations and controlled permissions.
  - heading "Start with lookup and recurring questions" [level=2]
  - paragraph: "Review product manuals, service rules or internal instructions: who maintains them, which version is valid, who can access them and what source should support an answer."
  - paragraph: When information conflicts, is outdated or lacks evidence, the assistant should say it cannot confirm and refer the issue to a person. Keep important commitments and external responses subject to human review.
  - heading "What does a pilot need?" [level=2]
  - paragraph: Select authorized, redacted documents with known versions and representative questions. Separate questions that can be answered directly, need clarification or require human handling.
  - paragraph: Test retrieval, citations and answers first. For actions, assess interfaces, read and write access, confirmation, records and failure handling. Agree changes to existing systems separately.
  - heading "Assess evidence and behavior" [level=2]
  - paragraph: Use questions checked by business staff to assess retrieval, whether citations support the answer, outdated content handling and appropriate refusal when evidence is missing. Record error types before expanding.
  - paragraph: For actions, also test the target object, duplicate execution, recovery and human takeover. Model choice, data volume, integrations and maintenance affect cost and need separate agreement.
  - heading "Knowledge and support collaboration" [level=2]
  - paragraph: In the related RAG support project, I worked on knowledge preparation, retrieval configuration and testing. Illustrative answers are distinguished from project records. Tool-executing agents require a separate assessment.

  - heading "Questions before starting" [level=2]
  - heading "Do we have to train a model?" [level=3]
  - paragraph: Not necessarily. Assess existing models, retrieval and tool configuration against the task before investing in training.
  - heading "Can an agent handle customer work autonomously?" [level=3]
  - paragraph: Validate a specific task first. Orders, payments, commitments and data changes need defined permissions, confirmations and exception handling before deciding the automation level.
  - heading "Start with one task" [level=2]
  - paragraph: Describe the current process, its slowest step and the result you want.
